# ORB-15 — what to change to give it the best chance

Follow-up to `AUDIT-2026-08-30.md` (which was about what was *broken*). This is about what
should *change*. Two independent passes again: this one and Codex, which was given the
audit's corrections up front so it wasn't reasoning from the same false premises.

---

## 1. The reframe: this is a lottery-ticket strategy, and the ticket hasn't paid

New measurement, `scripts/orb-leverage-vs-friction.js`, n=949 cached real-option trades
paired with the underlying over the identical holding window, held to the flatten with no
stops:

```
option return:  mean +28.1%   MEDIAN -3.0%
  p10 -96%   p25 -44%   p75 +51%   p90 +159%   max +1196%
  51.6% of trades lose money
  the top 1% (10 of 949) contribute 9.6 of the 28.1 points of mean
```

Now put that next to live (n=50): **median -5.5%**, mean -3.62%, 70% losers.

**The typical live trade is behaving almost exactly as simulated** — sim median -3.0% vs
live median -5.5%. What live is missing is the *right tail*. The sim's entire profit comes
from it: p90 is +159%, and one trade in the sample returned +1196%. Live got exactly one
tail trade in 50 (TSLA, +148%).

So the -3.62% is not obviously a broken strategy. It is what this strategy looks like when
the tail has not shown up yet, and at n=50 that is unremarkable. This materially softens
yesterday's framing: the t = -5.24 against the backtest is still correct, but the mechanism
is most likely tail-absence, not a dead signal.

It also dictates the response: **survive to the tail, or don't play.** That is a sizing and
patience problem, not a parameter problem.

## 2. The friction arithmetic — how a real edge can still lose money

The only well-powered number in this repo is BACKTEST-BASELINE.md's **+8.1 bp/trade on the
underlying** (n=603). Every options figure (+2018bp, +3044bp, +2326bp) is that same edge
times option leverage, with fat tails and far less power.

Measured leverage: **48x** (regression through origin, n=949; median ratio 44.2x on trades
where the underlying moved > 0.2%). ATM premium is 1.04% of spot.

```
  +8.1bp underlying  x  48x leverage  =  +3.89% of premium per trade, GROSS

  round-trip friction as configured (entry limit at mid + 25% of half-spread,
  exit at MARKET paying the full half-spread):
      2% spread -> 1.25%   survives
      4% spread -> 2.50%   survives
      6% spread -> 3.75%   survives
      8% spread -> 5.00%   DOES NOT SURVIVE      <- MAX_SPREAD_PCT is 8
  break-even spread, ignoring theta entirely: 6.23%
```

And theta on top: on the 55 trades where the underlying moved **less than 0.1%**, the mean
option return was **-26.3%**. An underlying-only backtest scores those days as ~0.0bp. The
option holder pays for them.

This is the mechanism by which a genuine +8.1bp underlying edge produces a losing options
bot: at the widest spreads the gate admits, friction alone exceeds the entire gross
directional edge.

### Method note — a wrong analysis I ran and discarded
I first decomposed this as an OLS regression `option_return = alpha + beta x underlying_return`.
It returned **alpha = +23.4%, t = 6.2** — "holding a near-dated ATM option while the
underlying goes nowhere gains 23%" — which directly contradicts the model-free -26.3%
measured above. The regression is the one that's wrong: a long option's payoff is convex
(bounded at -100%, unbounded above), so a straight line through a convex cloud with a fat
right tail drags the intercept up and reports phantom positive carry. Removed from the
script, with the reasoning left in place so it isn't re-attempted. Mean-based statistics
are the wrong tool on this distribution — the same lesson this repo keeps re-learning.

## 3. Two hypotheses tested and killed

**Adverse selection on unfilled entries — FALSIFIED.** I claimed on 08-30 that the 22% of
entry orders that never fill are disproportionately the breakouts that ran, which would have
explained the missing tail *and* been fixable. Tested by pairing all 69 entry orders
FIFO-by-underlying against the underlying's subsequent move:

```
              n     max favourable excursion      close at flatten
  FILLED     54       +1.32% +/- 0.23              +0.56% +/- 0.23
  UNFILLED   15       +1.58% +/- 0.56              +0.10% +/- 0.44
  difference        +0.26pp  t=0.43              -0.46pp  t=-0.92
```

No effect. The unfilled signals ran marginally further on MFE (noise) and closed *worse*.
The missing tail is not an execution problem. (My first pass at this paired only 12 of 69
orders — it kept one pending order per symbol instead of a queue — and reported a spurious
"adverse selection confirmed". Fixed.)

**Trading the signal as shares — ALREADY SETTLED, don't revisit.** Codex correctly pointed
at `REPLACEMENT-2026-08-14.md` §"Rejected — ORB-15's signal traded as shares", which
verified out: its own 12 names +8.0bp (IS 8.3 / OOS 7.4), the other 44 names +3.2bp (does
not generalise), all 56 long-only after 5bp costs **-0.5bp**, account sim -8.9% to -18.2%.
Its conclusion stands: *"ORB-15 needs options leverage to be worth running. As shares it is
noise."*

## 4. Ranked: what to actually do

### 1. Log quotes at entry AND exit. (highest EV, costs nothing, unblocks everything)
The most promising cost change — tightening `MAX_SPREAD_PCT` from 8 — **cannot be validated
today**, and this is Codex's sharpest catch. `logs/option-cache-orb.json` holds OHLC bars,
not contemporaneous bid/ask. The 0/4/8% cost grid in the sweeps is a *modelled* cost, not a
measured spread. The trade log never recorded the spread actually accepted. So there is no
way to test "4% cap vs 8% cap" against the repo's own validation standard, and §2's
arithmetic is a hypothesis, not a validated defect.

Fix that first: record `bid`, `ask`, `spreadPct` at entry and at exit on every trade. In
~2 months it makes the single highest-value parameter question answerable with real data.
Everything else in this list is waiting on measurement; this is the one action that
manufactures it. **Do this now.**

### 2. Test the 11:00 entry cutoff (currently 11:30)
The strongest genuine-alpha candidate, and importantly **not newly mined**:
BACKTEST-BASELINE.md already documents `09:45–11:00 = +14.3bp` against +8.1bp pooled, and
`ORB_ENTRY_CUTOFF` was set to 11:30 anyway. Mechanism: avoid late, lower-quality breakouts
and the churn cost they carry.

*Validate:* run the existing option/ratchet sim on the cache with live ratchet, rel-strength
and cost assumptions matched. Select on the first chronological half, judge on the second, at
both 4% and 8% cost. *Falsify:* later-half net doesn't improve after costs, or the gain
vanishes when the best few trades are removed. **Do not sweep a grid of cutoff times** —
that is how this repo got its boundary optima.

### 3. Gate on the NET spread, not the per-leg spread (new finding)

`MAX_SPREAD_PCT` is enforced **per leg** (`lib/contracts.js:99`). For a two-leg debit
vertical the net bid-ask is the *sum* of both legs' absolute spreads, measured against a net
mid *smaller* than either leg. So two legs each comfortably inside the 8% cap routinely net
out far outside it:

```
  long leg           short leg          each leg     NET spread
  3.05/3.20 (4.8%)   1.45/1.55 (6.7%)   both pass      15.4%
  2.00/2.10 (4.9%)   0.95/1.02 (7.1%)   both pass      16.0%
  5.00/5.30 (5.8%)   2.60/2.78 (6.7%)   both pass      19.5%
```

On this path the gate does not bound what it appears to bound — and the spread path is used
precisely for the expensive names (TSLA/META/MSFT/COIN/MSTR) a small account cannot reach
with a single. Live points the same way, though n is small: **singles -2.02%/trade (n=44)
vs spreads -15.39%/trade (n=6)**, a 13.4pp gap at t≈1.13 — not significant, but the
*mechanism* is arithmetic, not statistical, so it does not depend on that t.

This is a different class of finding from "cut MAX_SPREAD_PCT to 4". That is a guess needing
data. This is a demonstrable gap between what a gate claims and what it does, and the fix is
not a new parameter — it is applying the existing one to the structure actually being traded.

*Done now:* `NET_SPREAD_OVER_CAP` is logged whenever both legs pass but the net breaches,
and `spreadPctAtOrder` records the net figure. **Warns, does not block** — net-spread gating
would silently drop the expensive half of the universe, and how many signals that costs
cannot be measured from an OHLC-only cache. Decide from the log after a few weeks.

### 4. Test skipping 0DTE as a filter
Live split is suggestive but weak — 0DTE n=32 at -4.38%/trade, 1DTE+ n=10 at +1.76%,
neither significantly different from zero (t = -0.68, +0.27). But §2's theta measurement
(-26.3% on a no-move day) is a real mechanism, and 65% of entries are 0DTE.

*Validate:* testable on the cache as a filter on the naturally-selected contract's expiry,
chronological holdout, 4%/8% cost grid. *Note the limit:* the cache cannot validate "buy the
1DTE **instead**" — it holds no alternate-expiry histories. A filter is testable; a
substitution is not. Don't deploy the latter.

### 5. Keep 5% sizing. Pre-register the evaluation window before looking at it.
Given §1 — median -3%, half the trades losing, profit in a tail — survival to the tail is
the entire game, and 15% did not provide it. Freeze the config now and pre-commit: N trades
(≥150 for a ±5pp interval at 32% sd; more, because trades cluster by day), judged on
**fill-based** P&L, which `EXIT.pnlSource` now provides for the first time. Decide at the
end. Do not re-decide weekly — that is how the last month was spent.

### 6. Accept the timeline, or stop deliberately
At 2.9 trades/session and 32.1% sd:

| edge to detect | trades needed (2 SE) | at current rate |
|---|---:|---:|
| +2%/trade | 1,031 | ~17 months |
| +3%/trade | 458 | ~7.5 months |
| +5%/trade | 165 | ~2.7 months |
| +20%/trade | 11 | ~4 sessions |

The backtest's +20% needed n=11 to reject, which is why it fell so fast. A realistic +3%
edge needs 7.5 months of trading to confirm. **That is the actual constraint on this
project**, and no parameter change moves it. More trades do not create edge; they only
measure it. If the frozen window comes back non-positive after costs, stop trading ORB-15
rather than searching parameters for a fourth time.

---

## 5. Explicitly do NOT do

| | why |
|---|---|
| Cut `MAX_SPREAD_PCT` now | Cannot be validated from OHLC bars. Do #1 first. |
| Marketable-limit exits | Would swap a bounded, known crossing cost for an **unbounded** no-fill window during 0DTE decay. Not a proven saving; needs quote-level study. (Codex) |
| Change the ratchet / `minStopRung` | Measured 08-30: expectancy flat across the whole grid, arming later strictly worsens drawdown. |
| Trade the signal as shares | Already tested and rejected — see §3. |
| Expand or prune the universe | Cache holds only these 12 names, so expansion cannot be honestly tested; pruning now would fit noise. |
| Chase trade frequency for its own sake | Frequency estimates the edge, it doesn't create one. |

## 6. The bottom line

Nothing found in two days of auditing creates an edge. What the fixes bought is that the
bot is now **honest** (P&L is the fill, not the mark), **hard to blow up** (a working
drawdown breaker, 5% sizing), and **doing what was backtested** (closed bars, not
provisional ones). That is the precondition for learning anything from the next 150 trades —
and until 08-30 none of it was true, which is why the last month taught nothing.

The single highest-value thing left is not a strategy change. It is recording the bid-ask
spread you are actually paying, because the one arithmetic that decides whether this
instrument can carry this edge (§2) currently has to be assumed.
