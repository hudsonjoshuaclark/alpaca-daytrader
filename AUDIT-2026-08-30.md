Triggered by "why am I losing money now". Two independent passes: this one, and a
read-only audit by Codex (GPT-5.2) over the same files. Where they agreed the finding is

Account: Alpaca paper **PA31D3KKVK1D**, equity **$2,252.36**, flat, no open positions.

## 1. The headline

The bot has never made money. The account reads $1,000 → $2,252, but the bot did not do
that: on 2026-08-03 three 0DTE calls were closed **manually** at 11:31 ET
(`MANUAL_CLOSE` ×3), taking equity $1,000 → $2,780 in one day. That single human
intervention is the entire gain.

Everything the bot has done unaided since then:

| | |
|---|---|
| Realised, 08-05 → 08-28 | **-$537** (equity 2780.72 → 2252.49) |
| Peak → now | $2,876.18 (08-21) → $2,252.36 = **-21.7%** |
| Completed trades | 50 |
| Win rate | 30.0% |
| Mean return / trade | **-3.62%** (sd 32.1%, SE 4.55%) |

`+$463` of the up-days is one trade — `TSLA260819C00342500`, +$460. Remove it and the bot
is **-$1,029 over 49 trades**.

### Where it goes

| Exit reason | n | win% | total | avg |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `option_stop` | 4 | 0% | **-$619** | -$155 |
| `or_mid_stop` | 8 | 13% | **-$538** | -$67 |
| `trail_stop` | 38 | 37% | +$588 | +$16 |

12 trades take -$1,157; the other 38 make +$588. Losers run to -55% or the OR-mid;
winners are cut by a 15% trail. That asymmetry is the shape of the loss.

## 2. The statistic the nightly review was not computing ✓✓

Eight consecutive reviews (08-21 … 08-28) tested **win rate** (31.25% live vs 40.2%
baseline ≈ 1.3 SE) and concluded "inside noise, no change" every night. That is correct
about win rate and useless: win rate has almost no power on a distribution whose edge
lives in rare +100% trades.

Tested on **expectancy** — the quantity position sizing is actually derived from:

```
live mean/trade      -3.62%   SE 4.55%
backtest claim      +20.18%   (sweep5-options.js)
t vs backtest         -5.24   p < 1e-6      <- decisively falsified
t vs zero             -0.80   noise
```

Both numbers matter and they say different things. **The live edge being negative is NOT
established** (t = -0.80; 50 trades cannot resolve a 32%-sd distribution). What *is*
established, overwhelmingly, is that the live process does not deliver +20%/trade — and
that claim was the sole justification for sizing at 15% of equity per trade.

**[codex]** The comparison is also not like-for-like. `sweep5-options.js` simulates only
`option_stop` / `or_mid_stop` / EOD and has **no ratchet**; live takes 38 of 50 exits via
`trail_stop` and zero via EOD, and substitutes debit spreads where the sim models a single
ATM option. So part of the gap is drift and part is model mismatch. Recorded in
AGENT-REVIEW.md; the baseline should be treated as an upper bound until a matching frozen
sim exists.

## 3. Bugs found and fixed

### 3.1 The drawdown circuit breaker could not fire — `lib/riskManager.js`
`PAUSE_DRAWDOWN_PCT` was measured against `initialCapital`, written **once** and never
updated. The file still held `1000` from the 08-03 reset, putting the floor at **$650**
against $2,252 of equity. A 21.7% peak-to-trough drawdown ran with the breaker showing
green; it would have taken a further **-71%** to trip.

A static anchor measures drawdown correctly only on the day it is set — after that it
measures return-since-inception. **Fixed:** the floor now trails a high-water mark, which
is what the parameter was always documented to mean. `peakEquity` seeded to the true
observed peak, $2,876.18 → floor $1,869.52.

### 3.2 Exit P&L recorded the mark, not the fill — `runner.js` ✓✓
Exits go out as **market** orders (fill at the bid); the P&L booked was the pre-order
`unrealized_pl` snapshot (marked at the **mid**). Every exit was recorded roughly half a
spread better than it actually filled, always in the same direction, on contracts
`MAX_SPREAD_PCT` permits to be 8% wide. Four consecutive nightly reviews measured the
overshoot at $2–15/trade and deferred it. That number fed `state.realizedPnL`, the daily
loss stop, and every expectancy figure the strategy is judged on.

**Fixed:** `closeTrade()` now reads the actual fill back off the close order and books
that. Falls back to the old snapshot if the fill can't be read (the order is already
submitted, so nothing here can delay an exit) and records which source was used in
`EXIT.pnlSource`; `pnlAtMark` is kept alongside so the gap is measurable directly.
Codex independently measured **$363.54** of recorded shortfall across 36 trail_stop exits.

### 3.3 Entries fired on bars that had not closed — `lib/marketData.js` **[codex]**
The freshness guard tested only the upper bound:

```js
if (Date.now() - barCloseMs > 6 * 60 * 1000) return null;   // rejects STALE bars
```

For a bar closing in the **future** that expression is negative, so it passed. The bot was
entering on provisional closes of still-forming bars — a signal the sweeps never simulated
(`sweep3.js` iterates finalized bars only).

Measured from `ENTRY_ORDER` timestamps: **44 of 69 entry orders (64%) fired more than 60s
into a 5-minute bar block**, the largest cluster (23) in the block's *last 30 seconds*.
Only 25 of 69 show the "acted on a just-closed bar" signature. This was the normal path.

**Fixed:** added `if (Date.now() < barCloseMs) return null;`. Regression test at
`scripts/test-forming-bar.js` (9 assertions, incl. that the upper bound still works).
`ENTRY_ORDER` now logs `barTime` and `barAgeSec`, which must never be negative again.

*Honesty note:* those trades did **not** perform worse (-0.67%/trade vs -9.35% for
closed-bar entries, t = 1.11, n = 50 — noise, and if anything the wrong direction). Fixed
because it makes the deployed strategy the backtested strategy, which is a precondition
for any comparison between them meaning anything — not because it demonstrably loses money.

### 3.4 A positions-fetch failure left every open option unmanaged — `runner.js` **[codex]**
`/v2/account` was carefully hardened to degrade rather than die (2026-08-10 DNS/TLS
outage). `/v2/positions`, three lines later, was unguarded — and a throw there reaches the
top-level catch, skipping `manageOpenTrades()` **and** the 15:45 flatten. The one endpoint
failure that leaves open 0DTE options completely unmanaged was the one still unprotected.

**Fixed:** falls back to the previous tick's snapshot for management (a stop on slightly
stale marks beats no stop check at all; exits are market orders and don't depend on the
price), logs `POSITIONS_UNAVAILABLE`, and suppresses **entries** while degraded since a
stale map cannot prove a position is absent.

### 3.5 Partial entry fills were abandoned — `runner.js` **[codex]**
`partially_filled` isn't in the `filled` branch, so such an order stayed `pending` until
the TTL, got cancelled, and then hit a branch that called `removeTrade()` on the basis of
status alone — ignoring `filled_qty`. The contracts were really owned: no `option_stop`, no
`or_mid_stop`, no ratchet, no concurrency slot, nothing to close them before the 15:45
untracked sweep. Never observed live (all 15 `ENTRY_UNFILLED` events had `filled_qty` 0),
but a multi-contract marketable limit is exactly the order type that partial-fills.

**Fixed:** any non-zero `filled_qty` is adopted as a managed position at the filled
quantity, logged as `ENTRY_PARTIAL_FILL`.

### 3.6 The smoke test paused the live bot — `scripts/smoke.js`, `lib/riskManager.js`
Found by running it. `scripts/smoke.js` calls `canEnterNewTrade(state, 1000, …)` with a
**hardcoded $1,000**, which reaches `checkAccountGuardrail()` — which *persists* what it
decides. It was harmless while the floor sat at $650; the moment the floor started trailing
a $2,876 high-water mark, the fake $1,000 tripped it and wrote `pausedForReview: true` to
the live file. AGENT-REVIEW.md step 6 tells the nightly agent to run smoke.js after **any**
code change, so the step that exists to prove a change is safe would have silently stopped
trading.

**Fixed:** `ORB_STATE_FILE` / `ORB_GUARDRAILS_FILE` env overrides; smoke.js points both at
a temp dir it deletes on exit. Verified: the live guardrail file is now untouched by a
smoke run.

## 5. Investigated and deliberately NOT changed

### 5.1 The ratchet's `minStopRung` — hypothesis measured and rejected
`lib/config.js` states a safety contract: *"this must not quietly introduce a tighter
entry-side stop than what was validated"*. That held when step == trail == 0.15 (rung 1's
stop sat at breakeven). Since the 08-04 change to step 0.02 / trail 0.15, rung 1 arms at
**+2%** of premium and sets its stop at **-13%** — and +2% is inside the quote noise of an
8%-wide contract. Live: 24 of 38 `trail_stop` exits are losses totalling -$660, armed at
rungs 1–8, with rung-1 advances logged at gains of 2.3–3.3%.

It looked like a regression worth reverting. New sweep `scripts/orb-ratchet-minstoprung.js`
(n=953 cached real-option trades, Jan–Aug 2026, chronological out-of-sample half):

```
out-of-sample, 4% round-trip cost     exp    maxDD  lossRate  avgLoss
  ratchet off                       1519bp   1465%    66.5%   -33.7%
  minStopRung 0                     1793bp    103%    43.4%   -13.2%
  minStopRung 1  (deployed)         1730bp    145%    40.3%   -17.2%
  minStopRung 8  ("restores" intent)1674bp    199%    34.0%   -26.1%
```

**The hypothesis was wrong.** Expectancy is flat across the whole grid (0…16) at every cost
level; only the *shape* of the losses changes, and arming later gives fewer, bigger losses
and a **worse** drawdown. Restoring the literal contract (rung 8) would make the bot worse.
The gradient mildly favours arming *earlier*, but rung 0 is the grid edge and the deltas
are small — the exact boundary-optimum signature this repo already learned to distrust.

**No change.** The stale comment in config.js is corrected instead, with the table.

### 5.2 The 15:45 sweep closes untracked positions — **[codex]**, downgraded
Codex flagged that `flattenAll()` market-closes every account position it doesn't track,
with no ownership filter, and rated it "unbounded". Correct about the code — but given §4
(ORB-15 owns its account alone) there is nothing else there to close, and 0 `FORCE_FLATTEN`
events exist in the log. A bot flattening everything in *its own* account at 15:45 is
defensible. Left alone; it becomes a real hazard only if a second strategy is ever pointed
at PA31D3KKVK1D.

### 5.3 Codex findings not acted on
- **Order-submission errors abort the tick** and retry the same signal (47 repeated 403s on
  08-03). Real, but the 403s were caused by 30%-of-equity sizing and haven't recurred since
  the cut to 15%; now 5%. Worth doing, not urgent.
- **`state.trades` counts pending orders**, so rollups report 62 "trades" against 48
  attributable wins/losses. Real accounting sloppiness; affects reporting, not decisions.
- **Four manual closures have no per-trade P&L**, so the live distribution is selected to
  bot exits and excludes the largest early gains. Correct, and it is why §1 separates the
  manual 08-03 day from the bot's own record.

Two things Codex checked and cleared: `OPTION_STOP_PCT` is correctly signed for both calls
and puts (all four live `option_stop` exits landed at -55.2% to -56.4%), and the entry-bar
`or_mid_stop` defect from the older reviews is genuinely fixed.

## 6. Sizing cut: 15% → 5%

`RISK_PCT_PER_TRADE` 0.15 → **0.05**, `DAILY_LOSS_STOP_PCT` 0.25 → **0.10**
(`scripts/risk-caps.lock.json` updated in the same change, per the contract in
`scripts/guard-config.js`).

The 15% figure was sized against the backtest's claimed edge, which §2 falsifies at
t = -5.24. config.js's own sizing study already worked out the consequence: at a
slightly-negative edge, 15% → -89%, 5% → -44%. Sizing is the one decision that must not be
made on the optimistic reading of an ambiguous edge. 5% is that study's own next rung and
keeps peak exposure near 20% of equity.

The daily stop at 25% never bound once: 08-26 lost **16.4%** of day-start equity and kept
trading. A stop that has never bound on the worst day it has seen is not a circuit breaker.
